> **Key Takeaways**
> - Pronunciation is one of **four equally weighted** IELTS Speaking criteria — worth a full 25% of your score.
> - Examiners assess **word stress, sentence stress, intonation and connected speech** — not whether you sound British or American.
> - A flat, **monotone** delivery is the single most common reason clear English gets stuck at Band 6.
> - You don't need a "perfect" accent. You need to be **easy to understand** and to use natural rhythm.
> - The fastest gains come from **recording yourself** and **shadowing** native speakers — not from memorising answers.

Most candidates pour their energy into vocabulary and grammar, then quietly lose half a band because of how they *sound*. If you've ever been told your English is "good" but your Speaking score won't move past 6, pronunciation is almost certainly the leak. The good news: improving pronunciation for IELTS Speaking is one of the most trainable parts of the whole exam — once you know exactly what the examiner is listening for. Below are seven specific fixes, each targeting a feature the band descriptors actually reward.

## First, Understand What "Pronunciation" Means in IELTS

Pronunciation is one of four criteria in IELTS Speaking, alongside Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy. Each carries equal weight, so a weak pronunciation score drags your overall band down by a quarter.

Crucially, the examiner is **not** grading your accent. They assess whether your speech features — individual sounds, stress and intonation — *help* or *hinder* communication. An accent is completely fine as long as it doesn't make you hard to follow.

Here's the difference a band makes in practice:

| Feature | Band 6 | Band 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Overall clarity | Generally understood, but mispronunciations cause occasional strain | Clear throughout, only minor lapses |
| Intonation & stress | Limited or inconsistent | Good control, used to support meaning |
| Connected speech | Words often spoken separately | Natural linking and contractions |
| Effect on listener | Listener must work at times | Effortless to follow |

## Fix 1 — Kill the Monotone (Use Intonation)

The biggest mistake candidates make is speaking in a flat monotone — especially when nervous. It makes even excellent answers sound rehearsed and mechanical, and it's a fast track to a capped score.

Intonation is the rise and fall of your voice. Let it rise on questions and key words, and fall at the end of statements. When you give an opinion, lean into the word that carries your feeling: "It was *absolutely* exhausting."

> **Pro tip:** Record a 60-second answer, then listen back with your eyes closed. If it sounds like a robot reading a list, your intonation — not your grammar — is what's holding you at Band 6.

## Fix 2 — Get Word Stress Right

English words have a stressed syllable, and putting it in the wrong place can make a common word sound foreign. Say *pho-TOG-ra-pher*, not *PHO-to-graph-er*; *com-FORT-able* collapses to three syllables in natural speech.

When you learn new vocabulary for IELTS, learn its stress pattern at the same time. A useful habit: tap the stressed syllable on the table as you say the word. It builds muscle memory fast.

## Fix 3 — Master Sentence Stress and Rhythm

English is stress-timed: you emphasise the words that carry meaning (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) and rush through the small grammar words (*the, of, to, a*). Candidates who give every word equal weight sound choppy and unnatural.

Practise by reading a sentence aloud and deliberately punching only the content words: "I **really** **enjoy** **travelling** to **new** **cities**." The rhythm that creates is exactly what examiners mean by natural sentence stress.

## Fix 4 — Use Connected Speech

At Band 7 and above, speakers naturally link words together rather than firing them off one at a time. This is **connected speech**, and it's a hallmark of fluent-sounding English:

1. **Linking** — "an_apple" becomes one smooth unit.
2. **Contractions** — say "I'd" and "they're," not "I would" and "they are."
3. **Weak forms** — "a cup *of* tea" sounds like "a cup *uh* tea."

You don't have to force these. Listen for them in natural English and let them creep into your speech — they're a sign you're speaking in phrases, not words.

## Fix 5 — Sharpen the Sounds That Trip You Up

You don't need every sound to be perfect, but a few high-frequency ones cause real confusion. The classic is the long /iː/ ("sheep") versus the short /ɪ/ ("ship"). Mixing them up can change your meaning entirely.

Identify the two or three sounds your first language doesn't have, and drill **minimal pairs** (ship/sheep, bat/bad, vest/west). Five focused minutes a day beats an hour of unfocused talking.

## Fix 6 — Record Yourself and Self-Assess

Your ears lie to you in real time — you can't fix what you can't hear. Recording yourself is the single most effective pronunciation tool you have.

- **Do this:** Record an answer, then compare it line by line against a model answer or native speaker.
- **Not that:** Practising silently in your head, or only ever speaking without listening back.

Track one feature per session — intonation today, word stress tomorrow — so you're not overwhelmed.

## Fix 7 — Shadow Native Speakers Daily

Shadowing means playing a short clip of natural English and repeating it *in real time*, copying the speaker's rhythm, stress and melody as closely as you can. It trains all the features above at once and builds confidence under pressure.

Use BBC Radio, podcasts, or short interview clips. Even having authentic English on in the background helps you absorb its rhythm and intonation over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does my accent affect my IELTS Speaking score?**
No. Examiners do not penalise accents. They only care whether your pronunciation is clear and easy to understand. An accent only matters if it makes you genuinely hard to follow.

**How long does it take to improve IELTS pronunciation?**
With focused daily practice — recording, shadowing and minimal-pair drills — many candidates hear a noticeable difference in three to four weeks. Deeply ingrained habits simply take a little longer.

**Is pronunciation really worth 25% of my Speaking score?**
Yes. It is one of four equally weighted criteria, so neglecting it caps your overall band no matter how strong your vocabulary and grammar are.

**What's the fastest single fix for a higher pronunciation band?**
Adding intonation. Moving from a flat monotone to a natural rise and fall is the change examiners notice most quickly.

## Final Thought

Improving your pronunciation for IELTS Speaking isn't about erasing your accent — it's about being clear, using natural stress, and letting your voice rise and fall the way fluent speakers do. Pick one fix from this list, work on it this week, and record yourself as you go. Small, steady changes add up quickly — and Band 7 is closer than it feels right now. You've got this.
