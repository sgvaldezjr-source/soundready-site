You've studied vocabulary lists. You've practised grammar drills. You've written essayafter essay, and yet your IELTS Writing Task 2 score refuses to budge past Band 6. You may even have followed popular IELTS Writing Task 2 tips that recommended more vocabulary or stricter grammar — and still the needle hasn't moved. Here's the truth: the real culprits are almost always Task Response and Coherence, two criteria that have nothing to do with language sophistication and everything to do with how you plan and organise your answer.

 

# **The Four Criteria Your Examiner Uses** 

Every IELTS Writing Task 2 essay is assessed on four equally weighted criteria, eachcontributing 25% of your band score:

\-Task Response (TR): Did you fully answer the question and take a clear, consistent position?

\-Coherence and Cohesion (CC): Is your essay logically organized and does it flow naturally?

\-Lexical Resource (LR): Do you use a range of vocabulary accurately and appropriately?

\-Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA): Do you use varied sentence structures with minimal errors?

 Most candidates dedicate the majority of their preparation to LR and GRA — the language-based criteria. But here is the critical insight: if you're stuck at Band 6, you are almost certainly losing points in TR and CC. Not vocabulary. Not grammar. The criteria you've been ignoring are the ones holding you back.

 

# **WhyTask Response Is Costing You Points**

 Task Response measures whether you have actually answered the question asked. This sounds straightforward, but it is the single most common reason candidates plateau at Band 6.

Answering the wrong question. You read "Do the advantages outweigh thedisadvantages?" and write a balanced essay presenting both sides equally —without committing to a clear opinion. That is a Band 6 ceiling. The question asked for your position, not a neutral summary.

Under developed arguments. You state a point but never explain with a relevant example. Examiners call this "listing," and it signals shallow thinking that caps your TR score regardless of how accurate your grammar is.

Missing part of the question. Many Task 2 prompts have two distinct elements. If you address only one, your TR score drops immediately — no matter how polished your language is.

The fix: before you write a single word, spend five minutes identifying exactly what the question is asking. Write your main position in one sentence. Then, check that every paragraph in your essay directly supports that position. If it doesn't, cut it or rewrite it.

 

# HowCoherence and Cohesion Block Your Path to Band 7

One of the most persistent myths among Band 6 candidates is that filling an essaywith transition phrases — "Furthermore," "Moreover,""In addition to this" — will lift a Coherence score. The opposite is true. Overusing connective phrases is a textbook Band 6 marker. Examiners recognize mechanical cohesion immediately, and it signals that you're compensating for weak IELTS Writing Task 2 structure rather than demonstrating genuine logical flow.

 *What Band 7+ coherence and cohesion actually looks like:*

\-Each body paragraph has one central idea, clearly expressed

\-Every supporting sentence in that paragraph explains or evidences that idea —nothing else.

\-Linking language is varied and used naturally, not inserted

\-The overall argument moves logically from introduction to conclusion with no detours or contradictions.

 A practical test: after finishing each body paragraph, ask yourself whether every sentence contributes directly to proving your topic sentence. If one doesn't,it doesn't belong there.

 

# What About Vocabulary and Grammar?

Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy still matter — but if you're at Band 6, your English is already strong enough to communicate clearly. What you need is precision and range, not artificial complexity.

For Lexical Resource, focus on collocations rather than isolated words. Knowing that "conduct research" is correct while "do research" is borderline and "make research" is wrong will serve you far better than memorizing rare synonyms. Paraphrase the question in your introduction using genuinely different vocabulary and avoid repeating key terms across your essay — use reference words and natural synonyms instead.

 For Grammar, mix your sentence structures, but never sacrifice accuracy for complexity. A clear, correct simple sentence always scores higher than a tangled attempt at something elaborate. Tense consistency is equally important:choose your tenses early and maintain them throughout the essay.
