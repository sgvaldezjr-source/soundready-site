# IELTS Task 2: Opinion and Discussion Prompts

## Key Takeaways

- Opinion prompts ask you to take a clear position and defend it
- Discussion prompts ask you to present both sides, then judge which is stronger
- Misidentifying the prompt type costs you 2–4 band points
- Learning to spot the prompt in 30 seconds prevents writing the wrong essay

---

## Introduction

Most IELTS students learn one template and try to force every prompt into it. But IELTS asks four distinct prompt types, each with different requirements.

Two of the most common are Opinion (agree/disagree) and Discussion (discuss both sides). They sound similar, but they're not. Writing an Opinion essay when the prompt asks for Discussion will cost you marks, no matter how well you write.

This guide breaks down both types and teaches you the structure for each.

---

## Prompt Type 1: Opinion / Agree or Disagree

### What It Looks Like

- "Do you agree or disagree?"
- "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
- "Is this a positive or negative development?"

### What It's Asking

Take a clear position (agree, disagree, or partially agree) and defend it with 2–3 reasons and examples.

### Opinion Essay Structure

**Introduction** (50–80 words): Paraphrase + state your position clearly

**Body Paragraph 1** (100–130 words): First reason + explanation + example

**Body Paragraph 2** (100–130 words): Second reason + explanation + example

**Body Paragraph 3** (80–100 words, optional): Briefly acknowledge the opposing view, explain why yours is stronger

**Conclusion** (50–70 words): Restate your position + summarize reasons

### Example

**Prompt:** "Computers have made life better. Do you agree?"

**Your essay:** Agree, because computers improved communication, access to information, and work efficiency.

---

## Prompt Type 2: Discussion / Discuss Both Sides

### What It Looks Like

- "Discuss both sides of this issue."
- "Discuss both views and give your opinion."
- "What are the advantages and disadvantages?"

**Key phrase:** "Both sides" or "both views"

### What It's Asking

Present both perspectives fairly and equally, then decide which one is more convincing.

### Discussion Essay Structure

**Introduction** (50–80 words): Paraphrase + state you'll discuss both perspectives (don't reveal your opinion yet)

**Body Paragraph 1** (100–130 words): First perspective + 2–3 reasons + example

**Body Paragraph 2** (100–130 words): Second perspective + 2–3 reasons + example

**Body Paragraph 3** (80–100 words): Your conclusion—which perspective is stronger? Why?

**Conclusion** (50–70 words): Summarize both views + restate which is stronger

### Example

**Prompt:** "Tourism harms local cultures. Others say it brings economic benefits. Discuss both views."

**Your essay:** Explain cultural harm side (commodification, tradition erosion) → explain economic benefits side (jobs, infrastructure, income) → judge which matters more.

---

## Opinion vs. Discussion at a Glance

| Aspect | Opinion | Discussion |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Argue YOUR position | Present both sides, then judge |
| **Your side** | 80% of essay | 40% of essay |
| **Other side** | 20% (brief acknowledgment) | 40% (equally developed) |
| **Intro** | State your position | Present both, hide opinion |
| **Body paragraphs** | 2–3 for your view | 2 for each view + judgment |

## How to Identify the Prompt in 30 Seconds

**These words = OPINION:**
- agree/disagree
- to what extent
- positive/negative development
- do you think

→ Take one side. Argue it. Briefly acknowledge the other.

**These words = DISCUSSION:**
- discuss both sides
- both views/arguments
- both perspectives
- discuss both... and give your opinion

→ Present both sides equally. Then judge.

## Common Mistakes

### Opinion Prompts

**Mistake 1:** Writing like it's a discussion
- You present both sides equally when the prompt wants a clear position
- **Fix:** 80% your view, 20% acknowledge others

**Mistake 2:** Not taking a clear position
- "Some think X, some think Y, both make sense"
- **Fix:** Say in intro: "I agree" or "I disagree" or "I partially agree"

**Mistake 3:** Changing your position mid-essay
- Start by agreeing, but argue the opposite in body paragraphs
- **Fix:** Keep position consistent from intro to conclusion

### Discussion Prompts

**Mistake 1:** Discussing only one side
- Explain tourism benefits but forget cultural harm
- **Fix:** Have at least 4 paragraphs (intro + side 1 + side 2 + conclusion)

**Mistake 2:** Not giving a final judgment
- Discuss both sides but never say which is stronger
- **Fix:** In final paragraph, state: "While both arguments have merit, I believe X is more important"

**Mistake 3:** Presenting sides unequally
- 2 paragraphs on benefits, 1 on harm
- **Fix:** Give roughly equal space to each side

## Signposting Language

### Opinion Prompts
- "I strongly believe..."
- "In my view..."
- "The main reason is..."
- "However, some argue that..." (acknowledgment only)

### Discussion Prompts
- "One perspective is..."
- "Proponents argue..."
- "On the other hand..."
- "While this view has merit, I believe..."
- "The stronger argument seems to be..."

---

## FAQ

**Q: Can I say "I partially agree"?**
A: Yes. Explain which aspects you agree/disagree with.

**Q: Do I need to mention the opposing view?**
A: No, but brief acknowledgment strengthens your essay.

**Q: What if I don't have a strong opinion?**
A: Pick a side and commit to it for the essay.
