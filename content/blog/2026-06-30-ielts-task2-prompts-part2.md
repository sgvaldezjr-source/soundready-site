## Key Takeaways

- Problem-solution prompts ask you to identify causes and propose realistic solutions
- Advantages-disadvantages prompts ask you to weigh pros and cons, then judge which matters more
- Solution essays fail when students only identify problems without solving them
- Advantages-disadvantages essays fail when students forget to make a final judgment

---

## Introduction

You've learned about opinion and discussion prompts. Now master the other half: problem-solution and advantages-disadvantages.

These types require different thinking. With problem-solution, you're problem-solving practically. With advantages-disadvantages, you're weighing competing interests. Many Band 6 writers stumble because they don't realize the structure is different.

This guide breaks down both types and teaches you the structure that works.

---

## Prompt Type 3: Problem and Solution

### What It Looks Like

- "What are the causes of this problem?"
- "What solutions would you propose?"
- "What problems does X cause, and how can we solve them?"

### What It's Asking

Identify 2–3 root causes, then propose 2–3 realistic solutions. You're analyzing and problem-solving.

### Problem-Solution Essay Structure

**Introduction** (50–80 words): State the problem + preview causes and solutions

**Body Paragraph 1** (100–130 words): First cause + why it matters + example

**Body Paragraph 2** (100–130 words): Second cause + why it matters + example

**Body Paragraph 3** (100–130 words): First solution + how it addresses the problem + why it's realistic

**Body Paragraph 4** (80–100 words): Second solution + how it works + why it's practical

**Conclusion** (50–70 words): Restate problem + summarize solutions

### Example

**Prompt:** "Young people struggle with mental health. What causes this, and what are solutions?"

**Your essay:**
- Cause 1: Social media creates unrealistic expectations
- Cause 2: Academic pressure increases stress
- Solution 1: Schools offer free mental health counseling
- Solution 2: Employers provide mental health days and therapy access

## Prompt Type 4: Advantages and Disadvantages

### What It Looks Like

- "Discuss the advantages and disadvantages."
- "Do the benefits outweigh the drawbacks?"
- "Is this more positive or negative?"

### What It's Asking

Present pros and cons fairly, then decide: which side is stronger? Do advantages outweigh disadvantages?

### Advantages-Disadvantages Essay Structure

**Introduction** (50–80 words): Paraphrase topic + say you'll discuss advantages and disadvantages (don't reveal judgment yet)

**Body Paragraph 1** (100–130 words): First advantage + explanation + example

**Body Paragraph 2** (100–130 words): Second advantage + explanation + example

**Body Paragraph 3** (100–130 words): First disadvantage + explanation + example

**Body Paragraph 4** (80–100 words): Your judgment—do advantages outweigh disadvantages? Why?

**Conclusion** (50–70 words): Summarize pros and cons + restate final judgment

### Example

**Prompt:** "Remote work has advantages and disadvantages. Discuss."

**Your essay:**
- Advantage 1: Flexibility—better work-life balance
- Advantage 2: Cost savings—no commute, cheaper setup
- Disadvantage 1: Isolation—miss social interaction
- Disadvantage 2: Communication issues—harder to collaborate
- Judgment: Advantages outweigh disadvantages

## Quick Comparison

| Aspect | Problem-Solution | Advantages-Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Main task** | Identify causes + propose solutions | Weigh pros and cons + judge |
| **Body paragraphs** | 4–5 (causes + solutions) | 4 (2 pros + 2 cons + judgment) |
| **Final paragraph** | Emphasize solutions | State which side is stronger |

## Common Mistakes

### Problem-Solution Essays

**Mistake 1:** Only identifying problems, not solving
- "Society should help" instead of specific solutions
- **Fix:** Be specific: "Schools should hire counselors funded by education budgets"

**Mistake 2:** Proposing unrealistic solutions
- "Ban social media" or "eliminate all stress"
- **Fix:** Propose realistic, implementable solutions

**Mistake 3:** Not linking solutions to causes
- List causes and solutions separately
- **Fix:** Explain which solution addresses which cause

### Advantages-Disadvantages Essays

**Mistake 1:** Forgetting to judge which side is stronger
- Discuss pros and cons but never say which outweighs the other
- **Fix:** State clearly: "The advantages outweigh disadvantages because..."

**Mistake 2:** Unequal coverage
- 2 paragraphs on advantages, 1 on disadvantages
- **Fix:** Give roughly equal space to each side

**Mistake 3:** Not explaining why one side matters more
- "Advantages outweigh disadvantages" (no reasoning)
- **Fix:** Explain: "Because they improve daily life, while disadvantages can be solved through technology"

## Signposting Language

### Problem-Solution
- "One major cause is..."
- "A contributing factor is..."
- "A practical solution would be..."
- "This approach would address the problem by..."

### Advantages-Disadvantages
- "A key advantage is..."
- "One benefit is..."
- "On the downside..."
- "A notable drawback is..."
- "Overall, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages because..."

## FAQ

**Q: Can I propose 4 solutions instead of 2–3?**
A: Yes, but aim for depth. One well-explained solution beats four shallow ones.

**Q: What if advantages and disadvantages are equal?**
A: You can argue they're balanced, but examiners prefer a judgment. If equal, explain why clearly.

**Q: Can I propose solutions not currently used?**
A: Yes, if they're realistic: "Governments could establish free mental health centers" is valid.
