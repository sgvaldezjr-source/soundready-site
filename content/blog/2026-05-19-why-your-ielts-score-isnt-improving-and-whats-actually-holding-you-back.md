So you've taken the IELTS more than once and your score hasn't budged. And you're probably sitting there thinking, "I just need to put in more hours."

Honestly? That's probably not it.

I've worked with a lot of IELTS candidates and the ones who get stuck aren't the ones who don't care — they care a lot. They're just grinding at the wrong stuff. So let me break it down.

You're practising but you're not actually learning

You do a practice test, check the answers, move on. Repeat. But that's not really studying — that's just taking the test over and over and crossing your fingers.

The real work happens after you get something wrong. That's where you stop, dig in, and figure out why. What did you miss? Where did your thinking go sideways? Skip that part and you're basically leaving all the useful stuff behind.

You're studying English. The exam doesn't care.

Here's the thing — IELTS isn't testing your English in general. It's a very specific exam and the examiners are looking for very specific things. I've seen people with solid English underperform because they treated it like a language class instead of learning how the exam actually works.

The band descriptors, the scoring criteria, the way questions are built — you can learn all of that. And once you do, the whole thing starts to make a lot more sense.

You're dodging your weak spot

Come on, you know which one it is. The skill that makes you uncomfortable — so you quietly spend more time on the stuff you're already okay at.

Nervous about Speaking? Bet you're not practising it enough. Hate Writing? You're probably doing extra Listening instead. Meanwhile that one weak skill is quietly pulling your score down every time.

And btw — for a lot of visa and university requirements, one low score can knock you out entirely, even if everything else looks fine. So you can't just ignore it.

You're going it alone

This is the big one. You can get yourself so far on your own — better vocabulary, faster reading, sharper listening. But if nobody's telling you that your Writing is all over the place or that your Speaking answers keep going off track, you'll just keep doing it wrong without realising.

Good feedback isn't just nice to have. It's kind of the whole thing.

So what do you actually do?

Look at your last practice test properly. Not just what was wrong — what keeps being wrong. Spot the pattern.

Read the band descriptors. Seriously. Know what a Band 7 answer actually looks like and why it scores what it scores.

Work on your weakest skill first. Give it more time, not less. It's uncomfortable for a reason.

Get someone in your corner who knows this exam. Not just to correct you — to show you what the examiner is seeing.

Look, being stuck doesn't mean you're not good enough. It usually just means the approach needs a tweak. And that's actually the easier fix.
