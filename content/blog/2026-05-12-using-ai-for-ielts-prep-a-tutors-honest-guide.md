Every IELTS candidate I work with asks me about AI now. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — can they really help you prepare? The answer is yes, but only if you use them properly. Most students use AI in ways that feel productive but don't actually move their band score. Here's what works.

**Writing Feedback That Actually Helps**

AI is genuinely useful for writing practice — but the prompt matters more than the tool.

Don't ask "is this good?" You'll get vague compliments that teach you nothing.

Try this instead:

"Score this Task 2 essay using the IELTS Writing band descriptors. Identify what I did well and where I'm losing marks. Be specific about which descriptor I'm failing."

The difference is huge. The first prompt gets you encouragement. The second gets you a diagnosis.

**Topic-Based Vocabulary**

Most students grind through generic word lists. This wastes time. The IELTS exam covers specific topic areas — environment, health, technology, education, urbanisation, work — so study vocabulary that maps to those.

Try this prompt: "Give me 10 advanced vocabulary words used in IELTS Writing about \[topic]. Use each in a sentence. Then quiz me with fill-in-the-blank questions."

You can do this on your phone — on the bus, at lunch, before bed. Five minutes of focused practice beats an hour of passive list-reading.

**Speaking Practice When You're Alone**

Practising speaking alone used to be impossible. Now it isn't.

Use ChatGPT voice mode or Gemini Live. Ask it to give you IELTS Speaking Part 1, 2, or 3 questions. Answer out loud. Then ask: "How was my fluency, vocabulary range, and grammar accuracy? What would an IELTS examiner flag?"

It feels awkward at first. Get past that — imperfect practice beats no practice.

**Understanding, Not Just Answering**

When you get a Reading or Listening question wrong, do NOT just check the answer and move on. That's the single biggest waste of study time I see.

Instead, paste the passage into Claude or ChatGPT and ask: "Why is the correct answer right? Why was my answer wrong? What did I misread?"

You can also ask AI to teach you the topic background. If the passage is about climate change, ask AI to explain why it matters, who's affected, and what governments are doing about it. That background knowledge transfers to every future passage on related topics.

**Examples That Make Words Stick**

When you learn a new word, don't memorise a definition — collect examples.

Learning "mitigate"? Ask: "Show me three real-world examples of how 'mitigate' is used in contexts that might appear in IELTS Writing."

Now the word is connected to something real. You'll actually use it in your essay instead of forgetting it.

**What AI Cannot Do**

I have to be honest about the limits.

AI cannot replicate real exam pressure. It will score your writing more generously than a trained examiner. It doesn't know your specific weak areas unless you tell it. And it will hallucinate — confidently giving you wrong information about the test format.

So use AI as a study partner, not a replacement for real preparation.
